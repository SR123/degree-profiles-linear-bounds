\title{Degree profiles of uniform polynomial consequences\\[3pt]
\large Linear lower bounds for Nullstellensatz and Polynomial Calculus}
\author{S\o ren Riis}
\date{}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Riis's LICS~2008 paper and expanded 2009 preprint established a four-way
algebraic proof-complexity classification for uniform polynomial
refutations in positive characteristic~\cite{Riis,RiisLong}. Its four
cases are mutually exclusive and exhaustive: $\mathrm T_1$ has eventually
bounded degree; $\mathrm T_2$ has no recurring finite degree;
$\mathrm T_3$ has finitely many recurring finite degrees without eventual
boundedness, with constant degrees on some residue classes and growing
degree on the others; and $\mathrm T_4$ has infinitely many recurring
finite degrees. The aim was to obtain polynomial-versus-exponential
complexity distinctions within this classification. The earlier degree
lower bounds were $\Omega(\log n)$ for Nullstellensatz and
$\Omega(\log\log n)$ for Polynomial Calculus; a conjectured improvement
was $\Omega(n^\varepsilon)$ for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$, at least for
Nullstellensatz and possibly for Polynomial Calculus.

This paper proves optimal linear degree lower bounds $\Omega(n)$ for
both systems and extends the theorem to uniform polynomial targets over
any field of fixed positive characteristic. The constant target $1$
gives the refutation case; the theorem applies more generally to uniform
polynomial targets. The lower bounds hold at all sufficiently large
sizes in $\mathrm T_2$, on the hard residue classes in $\mathrm T_3$,
and at arbitrarily large sizes with finite degree in $\mathrm T_4$.
The last case also has a finite liminf: one finite degree recurs
arbitrarily late, while recurring finite degrees are unbounded.
For finite derivations represented by full, explicit coefficient lists,
including zeros, this gives polynomial upper bounds in $\mathrm T_1$,
exponential lower bounds in $\mathrm T_2$, the corresponding periodic
mixture in $\mathrm T_3$, and polynomial subsequences with finite
exponential peaks in $\mathrm T_4$.
Degree-preserving lifting with linear onset and period bounds proves the
quantitative result; a separate uniform comparison gives NS and PC the
same type. A common $p$-adic hardness spectrum organises the eventual
bounded-degree residue classes and describes the four cases
topologically. Distinguishing any two types
is undecidable already for refutations. The central linear-bound,
classification, comparison and undecidability theorems are formalised
in Lean.
\end{abstract}
